# The wireframe — where disagreement is cheap

Step 4 is the hard gate that carries the constitution. A wireframe agrees on structure and core
function while nothing is polished, so changing an answer costs one gray box; after step 5 the
same change costs a redraw. Everything expensive about design gets decided here, in the cheapest
possible material.

## What a wireframe is made of

| In | Out (越阶 — flag it if you catch yourself) |
|---|---|
| gray boxes on a neutral ground | any non-gray fill, gradient, or image |
| real hierarchy: position, size, order | shadows, radii tuning, borders as decoration |
| **real copy** — actual headlines and labels, never lorem | brand typefaces; one neutral face, size and weight only |
| flows: what each control leads to | motion, hover states, empty-state art |

Real copy is non-negotiable: words are design material, and a structure approved over lorem is a
structure nobody approved. Type size and weight are allowed because hierarchy *is* structure;
everything else about type waits.

## The gate transcript

At the wireframe, the two questions from design/composition get asked **out loud, per element**:

1. Has it earned its way in — who does this serve? (no answer, it goes, now, while it's a box)
2. Which five things get the permanent place? (the rest queue behind interaction or the cut)

Write the answers next to the boxes. This transcript *is* the gate evidence: the owner approves
a wireframe plus its answers, not a picture alone. An approval with no transcript is what the
gate audit flags as evidence-free.

## Passing the gate

Present the wireframe with: the brief's success criterion, the chosen direction sentence, the
per-element transcript, and the one thing you're least sure about (name it — don't make the
owner find it). "Structure agreed" from the owner opens step 5. Tension you couldn't resolve
does not get carried silently into the draft; it's either settled at the gate or written down
as an open decision with an owner.

## Never

- Enter step 5 with an unapproved wireframe, or approve one for the owner in your head.
- Polish a box to "help them imagine it" — a prettier wireframe is a worse instrument; its job
  is to keep the argument about structure.
- Redesign the structure during step 6 iterations. If iteration reveals the structure is wrong,
  come back through this gate; that costs less than pretending it didn't.
