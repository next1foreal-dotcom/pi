# The brief — the first limit

Step 1 turns an idea into one page. Nothing visual exists yet, and that is the point: the brief
is the constitution's first application — **scope is the first detail budget**. Every element
that later faces the entry test ("who does this serve?") traces its answer back to a line here.
No line, no element.

## What a brief holds

| Field | One or two sentences |
|---|---|
| Problem | what is broken or missing, in the user's words, not the solution's |
| For whom | the person and the moment — "Fei, glancing at the pane mid-task", not "users" |
| Success when | an observable outcome — what they can do, find, or feel that they couldn't |
| Constraints | the project's tokens and components (the system is the specification), platform, deadline |
| Out of scope | named exclusions — the things this design will *not* do, written down so they stay out |
| Direction hint | only if the owner gave one; quote it verbatim, never paraphrase a taste |

Six fields, each one or two sentences. A brief that runs past a page is a design hiding in prose.

## Writing it from a one-line ask

The owner says "make a settings page". Draft the brief yourself — filling gaps is your job,
returning a questionnaire is not. Mark every value the owner didn't give as your default
(**"我垫的"** — visible, reversible), then show the page for a soft nod before research starts.
Ask a question only when the answer would change the design; everything else, decide and mark.

## Success criteria are the sharpest knife

"Looks clean" is not success. "Fei finds the running task in under three seconds without
scrolling" is — it will later reject a hero image, a second column, a decorative chart, without
any argument about taste. Write success so that it can *cut*.

## Never

- Start research without a success criterion — research without a target collects everything.
- Let the brief grow silently mid-project. A scope change is a decision; decisions belong to the
  owner and get a line in the brief with a date.
- Write the brief after the design to justify it. The brief predicts; it does not excuse.
