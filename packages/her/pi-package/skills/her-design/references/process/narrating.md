# Process: Saying What You Are Doing

He can see your edits. He cannot see your reasoning. `her_status` is the only bridge, and an
unnarrated stretch of work reads as either a stall or a surprise — both of which cost him the
ability to steer you before the work is finished.

## When

- **When you start on something**, not after.
- **When your focus shifts** — a new screen, a new direction, moving from structure to detail.
- **When you finish or hand back.**

One update per *phase of work*. Not one per tool call: a status that changes every few seconds
is a progress bar, and a progress bar tells him nothing he can act on.

## What a good headline says

Present tense, one line, specific enough that he could object to it.

| | |
|---|---|
| ✗ | 在做设计 · 处理中 · 优化界面 |
| ✓ | 在收紧 product-list 的卡片间距,从 16 改到 24 |
| ✓ | 刚把 hero 换成编辑式排版,想听你一句再往下铺 |

The test: **could he disagree with this sentence?** "在优化界面" cannot be disagreed with, so it
carries nothing. "把 hero 换成编辑式排版" can — and if he disagrees, you just saved a page.

## `waiting` is not for politeness

Set it only when you genuinely cannot proceed without him — a direction he has to pick, a
tradeoff only he can make, a taste question with no defensible default. It lights the panel up
as "waiting on you", so using it to be deferential trains him to ignore it.

If you can pick a sane default and say which one you picked, do that instead and keep going.
That is the whole difference between asking and stalling.

## The name is a title, not a log line

The first `name` you set is what this session is called in his panel for the rest of its life.
Name it after the *work* — "product-list 的呼吸感", "loora landing 三个方向" — not after the
step you happen to be on.
